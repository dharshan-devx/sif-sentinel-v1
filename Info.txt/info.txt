33. What I would NOT do
❌ Don't scrape random Google articles

Articles aren't structured training data.

Your system needs to distinguish precursors, not merely fatalities.

❌ Don't call all near misses SIF

A near miss can be low potential or high potential.

❌ Don't call all severe injuries SIF precursors

They're actual outcomes, not necessarily precursor observations.

“The domain has structured entities and relationships—reports, sites, activities, hazards, barriers and rules—so PostgreSQL provides strong relational consistency while still supporting flexible fields through JSONB where necessary.”

29. Why Not LLM Everything?

This is the heart of the project.

Normalize reports into:

34. Risk Score

Don't simply use:

number of reports

because a site with more reports isn't automatically more dangerous.

Build a composite score.

For example:

Risk Score =
    0.40 × SIF Density
  + 0.25 × SIF Frequency
  + 0.20 × Barrier Failure Rate
  + 0.15 × Recency

Normalize each component to 0–1.

Then:

0.75 – 1.00 → HIGH
0.50 – 0.74 → MEDIUM
0.00 – 0.49 → LOW

These weights are your prototype design choices, not OIL-prescribed values. Make that explicit.

74. Judge Question — Dataset
Judge:

Where did you get the data?

Answer:

"For our prototype, we use publicly available safety narratives and domain references together with clearly identified synthetic or de-identified OIL-style reports. We don't represent synthetic data as OIL data. A production deployment would use authorized OIL HSSE data."

75. Judge — Why SIF?

"Because actual injury severity doesn't necessarily represent the potential severity of the event. A near miss with a failed critical barrier can have high SIF potential even when nobody was injured."

76. Judge — Why AI?

"The reports are primarily free text. Traditional structured analytics cannot reliably identify activity, hazard, barrier failure and SIF potential from unstructured narratives. NLP allows us to convert that text into structured safety intelligence."

77. Judge — What if AI is Wrong?

"We use confidence thresholds and human-in-the-loop review. Low-confidence or ambiguous reports are routed to HSE personnel instead of being treated as authoritative decisions."

78. Judge — Why LLM?

"We don't use the LLM as the sole decision maker. We use a hybrid architecture where statistical classification, structured extraction, domain-constrained Life-Saving Rule mapping and human review work together."

79. Judge — How Do You Detect Patterns?

"We normalize each report into activity, hazard, barrier and barrier-failure attributes, then aggregate recurring combinations across sites and time. We calculate SIF density and a prototype risk score to prioritize patterns."

80. Judge — Can This Scale?

Answer:

"Yes. The architecture separates ingestion, AI inference, persistence and analytics. PostgreSQL handles structured analytical data, while the AI pipeline can be scaled independently. For production, the inference layer could be deployed as a separate service."

81. Judge — What Happens With Confidential OIL Data?

"The production architecture is designed for private deployment. Data doesn't need to leave OIL's controlled environment. The prototype demonstrates the intelligence layer without claiming access to confidential OIL data."

Excellent answer.
